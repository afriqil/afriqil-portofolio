What I Did

End-to-end fraud and compliance analytics workflow.

I analyzed 6,362,620 synthetic mobile-money transactions containing transaction type, amount, origin and destination balances, account identifiers, fraud labels, and the existing PaySim fraud flag. The dataset contains 8,213 simulated fraud cases, producing an observed fraud rate of approximately 0.129%.

I performed data-quality checks, exploratory analysis, and feature engineering in Python. The engineered features included transaction-to-balance ratios, balance-depletion behavior, transaction frequency, receiver-side velocity, high-value transaction indicators, balance discrepancies, and transaction-channel risk.

I then developed an explainable rule-based risk-scoring framework, compared it with the existing PaySim flag, Logistic Regression, and Isolation Forest, and exported a processed dataset for Power BI. The dashboard evaluates total activity, fraud concentration, high-risk alerts, precision, recall, false positives, false negatives, and a high-priority investigation queue.

Key Insights

What the analysis revealed.

Fraud was extremely rare

The dataset contained 8,213 fraud labels across 6.36 million transactions, resulting in an observed fraud rate of approximately 0.129%. This makes accuracy unsuitable as the primary evaluation metric.

Fraud concentrated in specific channels

Simulated fraud activity was concentrated in TRANSFER and CASH_OUT transactions, making transaction type an important risk signal.

The initial rules generated excessive alerts

A broad initial rule design produced millions of alerts. Revising balance logic, tolerance thresholds, risky channels, transaction ratios, and receiver velocity reduced the high-risk queue to approximately 27,000 transactions.

Recall and workload must be evaluated together

The revised rules achieved approximately 97.5% recall on the synthetic fraud labels, but false positives remained materially larger than true positives. High recall alone does not establish operational effectiveness.

Explainability supported investigation prioritization

Rule-based scores made the investigation queue easier to interpret than a black-box prediction alone, while machine-learning models provided useful performance benchmarks.

Recommendations

Operational implications from the analysis.

Prioritize TRANSFER and CASH_OUT activity

Focus review resources on high-value transfers, cash-out transactions, and transactions that rapidly deplete the sender's balance.

Evaluate precision and recall together

Monitor both fraud capture and false-positive workload when adjusting thresholds. A high recall rate should not be considered sufficient on its own.

Review false-positive patterns regularly

Analyze false positives by transaction type, amount range, risk score, and account behavior to identify overly broad rules.

Use risk-based investigation prioritization

Review higher-scoring alerts first, especially where large transaction amounts, balance depletion, and receiver velocity occur together.

Integrate customer-risk information in practice

A real AML system should combine transaction signals with KYC risk, geography, sanctions screening, customer profiles, and investigation outcomes.

Methodological Note

How the results should be interpreted.

PaySim contains synthetic transactions and simulated fraud labels. The AML risk categories in this project are analytical alerts for investigation prioritization, not confirmed criminal activity.

The dataset does not include customer KYC profiles, geographic exposure, sanctions screening, source-of-funds information, cross-institution networks, or real case-investigation outcomes. The project should therefore be interpreted as a portfolio demonstration of transaction-risk analytics rather than a production AML compliance system.
